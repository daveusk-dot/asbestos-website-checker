# Where we obtained your details

UK GDPR Article 14 applies because these work addresses are taken from a directory or a published page, not from the person. Say the source in the email itself. Do not replace it with "publicly available information" and nothing more.

## Line

```
Where we obtained your details: the work contact details for {{FIRM_NAME}} were taken from {{SOURCE_DIRECTORY}} on {{SOURCE_DATE}}. They were not obtained from you directly.
```

## How to fill the source

{{SOURCE_DIRECTORY}} is the actual place the address was read. Examples of the kind of phrase that belongs here, once it is true for that firm:

- the UKAS list of accredited organisations
- the ATaC directory of members
- the contact page on the firm's own website
- both, if both were used: name each one

{{SOURCE_DATE}} is the date that page or directory entry was read, in the form 19 November 2026 or 2026-11-19. Use the real date. Do not leave a guessed date.

Do not name a directory the firm is not actually listed in. Do not use a bought list, a guessed `firstname.surname` address, or a personal webmail address. If the only published address is a personal inbox, do not email it under this project.

The same facts go in the firm list columns `email_source` and `source_directory`.
